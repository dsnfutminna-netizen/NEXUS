# NEXUS Student Survey (v1)

**Purpose (3 jobs at once):**
1. **Validate the problem** — do students actually experience this?
2. **Prioritise features** — what must v1 have?
3. **Recruit pilot testers + pre-seed profiles** — warm users for launch day.

**How to run it:** paste into Google Forms (free, fast). Target **80–150
responses** across levels/departments. Keep it under **7 minutes**. Put consent
first. Make only screening + a few core questions required.

---

## Section 0 — Consent (required)
> NEXUS is a student-built project by DSN FUTMinna. This survey is anonymous
> unless you choose to share your email for the pilot. Your answers will only be
> used to design the platform.

- [ ] I understand and agree to take part. *(required)*

## Section 1 — About you (profile seed)
1. Department — *dropdown (from `departments` list)*
2. Level — `100 / 200 / 300 / 400 / 500 / 600 / 700`
3. Which career are you most aiming toward right now? — *dropdown:*
   Data Scientist · AI Engineer · Software Developer · Product Designer ·
   Digital Marketer · Still deciding · Other
4. *(Optional)* Your current CGPA band — `4.50–5.00 / 3.50–4.49 / 2.40–3.49 / 1.50–2.39 / Below 1.50 / Prefer not to say`

## Section 2 — Problem validation *(the core of the survey)*
Rate 1 (Strongly disagree) – 5 (Strongly agree):
5. "I have a clear picture of what skills I need for the career I want."
6. "I know which of my skills are strong and which are weak for that career."
7. "I can easily find opportunities (internships, scholarships, hackathons) that are relevant to me."
8. "I know what I should focus on learning over the next 3 months."
9. "The information I need about my growth is scattered across many places."

10. In the last 3 months, how often did you look for an opportunity (internship, scholarship, competition, etc.)?
    `Never / Once / A few times / Many times`
11. When you look for opportunities, what is hardest? *(multi-select)*
    - Finding them at all
    - Knowing if I'm eligible
    - Knowing if they fit my goals
    - Keeping track of deadlines
    - Nothing, it's easy
12. *(Open)* What's the most frustrating thing about planning your own growth as a student?

## Section 3 — Solution & feature priority
> NEXUS would let you build a profile and get: a **skill-gap analysis** vs your
> target career, **matched opportunities**, and a **3-month roadmap**.

13. How likely would you be to use this? — 1 (Not at all) – 5 (Definitely)
14. For each feature, mark **Must-have / Nice-to-have / Don't need**:
    - Skill-gap analysis for my target career
    - Personalised opportunity matching
    - 3-month development roadmap
    - Academic analytics (GPA trends, weak courses)
    - Course intelligence (what a course is used for, tools, projects)
    - Community insights (what skills peers are building)
15. What would make you **come back weekly**? *(open)*
16. Which ONE feature, if missing, would make NEXUS not worth using? *(open)*

## Section 4 — Current behaviour (baseline)
17. What do you use today to plan your development / find opportunities?
    *(multi-select: WhatsApp groups · Twitter/X · LinkedIn · Google · Friends/seniors · Nothing structured · Other)*
18. Have you ever missed an opportunity because you found out too late?
    `Yes / No / Not sure`

## Section 5 — Pilot recruitment (optional)
19. Would you like to be one of the first testers of NEXUS?  `Yes / No`
20. If yes, your email: *(short answer — stored separately from anonymous answers)*
21. Would you help by giving 15 minutes of feedback after trying it? `Yes / No`

---

## What we'll measure from this survey
- **Problem is real** if Q5–Q8 average **≤ 3** (students feel unclear) and Q9 is high.
- **Demand** if Q13 ≥ 4 for a solid majority.
- **Feature cut list** = anything mostly marked "Don't need" in Q14 → drop from v1.
- **Pilot list** = Q19/Q20 → these become launch-day users (and Q&A quotes).
- Q4 (CGPA band), Q1–Q3 → pre-seed realistic demo profiles.

> Tip: also run **5–8 short live interviews** with people who rated the problem
> highest. Surveys tell you *what*; interviews tell you *why*.
